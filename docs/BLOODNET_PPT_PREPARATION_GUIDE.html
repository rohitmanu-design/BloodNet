• Master Viva Q&A Cheat Sheet (Top 10 Questions with Simple Answers)

Q1: Why did you use WebSockets instead of regular HTTP requests?

Simple Answer: Regular HTTP requires the browser to constantly refresh or poll the server every few seconds, which is slow and wastes battery. WebSockets keep a permanent open channel, so the server can push an alert to the donor's screen in less than 180 milliseconds.

Q2: What is the Haversine formula and how does it work?

Simple Answer: Since the Earth is round, simple straight-line math (Pythagoras) is inaccurate over real distances. The Haversine formula uses spherical trigonometry and latitude/longitude coordinates to calculate the true surface distance between two points in kilometers.

Q3: How do you prevent fraud or fake donation certificates?

Simple Answer: Every certificate includes a unique SHA-256 cryptographic hash generated from the donor's ID, blood unit ID, and hospital signature. When anyone scans the QR code with their phone, it verifies this hash against our database. If anyone alters the certificate text, the hash won't match.

Q4: How do you protect patient and donor privacy?

Simple Answer: We follow a strict zero-knowledge policy. When a request is created, donors only see the hospital name and approximate distance. Phone numbers and live GPS coordinates are hidden until both parties accept. Furthermore, all active tracking links automatically expire after 24 hours.

Q5: What happens if two patients try to claim the last unit of O- blood at the same second?

Simple Answer: We use atomic MongoDB database transactions with locking. The first request decrements the inventory atomically. The second request is safely rejected with an "Out of Stock" notification, completely preventing double-booking.

Q6: Why did you add Google Maps navigation if you already have GPS?

Simple Answer: GPS coordinates often point to the center of a hospital building or an alleyway behind the emergency room. Our system uses verified street addresses and Google Maps turn-by-turn routing so drivers arrive directly at the emergency reception gate without wasted time.

Q7: What is the role of Artificial Intelligence in your project?

Simple Answer: As B.Tech AI & DS students, we structured BloodNet+ to gather live geographic and time-series donation data. In our Future Scope, we use predictive ML algorithms (Random Forest and LSTM time-series models) to forecast regional blood shortages 3 weeks ahead based on historical weather, festival, and dengue data.

Q8: Where is your project hosted and can we test it right now?

Simple Answer: Yes, sir. It is deployed live on Render Cloud at https://bloodnet-2.onrender.com/ with an automated CI/CD pipeline from GitHub. Any smartphone with internet can access it right now.

Q9: What was the hardest challenge you faced while building this project?

Simple Answer: The hardest challenge was synchronizing real-time Socket.IO events with geospatial distance filtering so that only compatible donors within traveling range received the alert, without overloading server memory.

Q10: What are the 8 standard blood groups managed in your hospital inventory?

Simple Answer: A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+, and O-. Our dashboard monitors all 8 groups in real time and automatically flashes critical warnings when any stock drops below 5 units.
